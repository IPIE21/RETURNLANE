# Returnlane — GM conversation guide

## The opening

“When a customer leaves after a trade-in appraisal, your salesperson has already done the hard work of building a relationship. Returnlane handles the follow-up that gets missed, gives the customer a modest, pre-authorized reason to return, and puts them back with that same salesperson.”

“This is a local demonstration. These are fictional customers and simulated messages. The offer rules and handoff workflow you are about to see are working code.”

## Demonstrate the promise

Import Sam Rivera through Start guided demo. Point to the original $32,900 appraisal, Jordan Ellis’s ownership, and the permission record. Click the first follow-up. The amount becomes $33,150, with a clear expiration and reinspection condition.

“The increase comes from the policy engine. The assistant cannot invent an amount or negotiate its way past the limit.”

Simulate interest, pick a return slot, and accept the handoff. Open Salesperson handoffs.

“Jordan gets the customer, their reply, the approved offer, and the visit time. The system brings the conversation back; Jordan handles the actual deal.”

## Demonstrate control

Open Offer safeguards and propose $1,500 above the original. Show the rejection and activity log. Explain the $500 outer ceiling, the lower per-lead limits, and the default $250 first offer. State plainly that these are prototype defaults, not a recommendation that the dealership adopt those exact amounts.

On another lead, simulate a customer asking for more money. Show the human-review handoff and unchanged quote. Simulate STOP and show that later replies cannot reactivate automation. Show Pause recovery.

“We should agree on the approved authority and escalation rules before a pilot. The demo makes those boundaries concrete.”

## Questions that make a pilot possible

1. Where is a completed trade-in appraisal stored, and how do you identify a visit that ended without a sale? Can the CRM produce a small export with visit time, appraisal, salesperson, contact details, contact permission, and outcome?
2. Who can approve an increase, how small should it be, when does it expire, and what requires reappraisal? Should some vehicles or margin situations receive no increase?
3. Where should an interested reply land so a salesperson actually sees and accepts it? Who covers an absent or departed employee?
4. Which messaging system and contact policy does the dealership already use? What evidence records permission and opt-out status?
5. What is a successful pilot: acknowledged handoffs, kept appointments, or sales confirmed in the CRM? What counts as a recovery, and how are existing active conversations excluded?

## A proposed pilot, subject to their answers

Start with one store, one approved lead category, and a dealer-agreed cap. First run in shadow mode: generate the intended queue and message previews for review, with no sending. After the store approves the data quality, offer authority, templates, and contact rules, introduce actual delivery with a named manager and salesperson escalation path.

Measure eligible visits, actual deliveries, replies, opt-outs, acknowledged handoffs, booked appointments, kept appointments, and dealer-confirmed sales separately. Compare against an agreed baseline or holdout when practical. Never call an appointment a recovered sale.

## Pricing conversation

Anchor the conversation with a **$299 per store, per month starter pilot** for an independent dealership validating one approved lead source, one SMS recovery sequence, offer-policy controls, and handoff reporting. State that final pilot scope still depends on volume and integration work. This gives a buyer a number to react to without pretending that every deployment costs the same.

An eventual store subscription is operationally simple. Outcome-based billing needs a shared definition of recovery and CRM evidence. A revenue-share structure adds attribution and reconciliation work that this demo does not solve.

The strongest next ask: “Can we review a sample of your unclosed trade-in visits and agree on the follow-up authority with whoever owns the CRM process?”
